One job: break the direction while it's still cheap to fix.

## Stance
Adversarial. Do not soften. The pair's attachment to the work is part of what's being tested.

## Process
1. **Load-bearing assumptions.** For each, what evidence exists? "None" = a risk to declare Friday.
   Test cheaply where the time allows.
2. **Pre-mortem.** It's 12 months on, this shipped and failed. Three most likely causes. Which
   could have been seen today?
3. **Motivation test.** Every step where a human must act: what's in it for them, at that moment,
   in their words? "They should want to" is not an answer.
4. **Mess test.** Overlapping domains · contractors · multiple entities · mergers · users in
   several companies · bad actors joining · one-person companies and 4,000-person companies ·
   migration of companies that already exist.
5. **Technical assumption check.** Where an assumption could materially change the direction, pull
   an engineer in briefly **now**. Do not carry an avoidable assumption into Friday.
6. **The sceptic.** Who will be most sceptical Friday, what's their objection, and are they right?
7. **Trust and policy.** This is company money. Where is the exposure — financial, policy, privacy,
   duty of care?

## Sort every finding
**fix now** · **declare as known risk** · **hand to Engineering** · **accept and say why**.
Anything unsorted will ambush the playback.

## Outputs
- `pressure-test.md` → `output/` — attacks run, what broke, what changed, risks classified,
  technical questions for Engineering

## Human check
Name the one thing that changed because of this stage. If nothing changed, say specifically why the
direction survived intact — and be suspicious of the answer.
