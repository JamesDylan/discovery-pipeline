# 01_frame — frame (fixture seed)

Synthetic. Written to be a *good enough* input for `02_explore` and `03_converge` — specific,
falsifiable, and opinionated enough that a downstream stage inheriting it has something to push
against.

## Problem statement

Nobody owns a credit, so nobody spends it: the traveller holds an asset they didn't pay for and
can't value, and the company paid for an asset it can't see.

## The problem behind the problem

Framing this as "travellers can't find their credits" makes it a visibility problem and produces a
balance screen. The evidence doesn't support that. One of nine travel managers could name their
outstanding balance — but the travellers who *were* shown a balance in the 2024 study still didn't
act on it, because spending a credit means accepting a worse itinerary on a carrier they didn't
choose, for a saving that lands on someone else's budget line.

So: an ownership and incentive problem wearing visibility's clothes.

## Whose problem is it

Ranked by who feels it:

1. **Finance** — real leakage, invisible on their reports, so it never becomes a priority.
2. **Travel manager** — feels it as reputational risk when it surfaces in an audit.
3. **Traveller** — does not feel it at all. This asymmetry is the whole problem.

## Customer outcome vs business outcome

- Customer: money already spent gets used, without anyone administering it.
- Business: a defensible reason for a company to route more volume through the product — recovered credit
  value is the most legible saving in corporate travel.

They align, which is unusual and worth exploiting.

## Evidence for

- 1 of 9 travel managers could name their balance
- Credit-related tickets are 3rd-highest handling time despite low volume
- 55–70% estimated expiry-unused rate

## Evidence against

- The expiry estimate is wide and partly derived, not measured. Two carriers report nothing.
- The traveller interviews predate the current flow. If the flow changed materially, the motivation
  finding may be an artefact.
- No evidence yet that companies would accept pooled credits — a legal and tax question nobody
  has asked.

## Load-bearing assumptions

1. Travellers will accept a constrained itinerary if the constraint is invisible at choice time.
   **If false, every option that nudges at booking fails.**
2. Credits can be legally re-pointed to another employee for at least the major carriers.
   **If false, pooling is dead and only traveller-level options survive.**
3. Finance would act on credit leakage if it appeared on a report they already read.
   **If false, this stays a traveller-motivation problem and the business case shrinks.**

## What we are explicitly not solving

- Recovering credits already expired.
- Negotiating better credit terms with carriers. Commercial, not product.
- Refund-eligible cancellations. Different mechanism, different problem.

## Open question for `02_explore`

Every framing above still assumes someone decides to spend a credit. The option set must contain at
least one where no one decides.
