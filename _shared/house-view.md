# House view — your theory of best

> **STATUS: EMPTY.** Until this is filled, every stage in this workspace runs on generic product
> best-practice, and generic input produces generic output. A pipeline that encodes no opinion
> cannot produce a point of view — and a point of view is the entire deliverable.
>
> This is the highest-leverage file in the workspace. It is also the only one nobody else can write.

## Why this exists

The structure is worth nothing on its own. What makes a folder pipeline produce good work is that
someone who has done the job long enough to know where it breaks built the folders around the
break point. That knowledge is not in the brief and not on the internet. It is a position formed by
doing the work, and it has to be written down to be usable by anyone but its owner.

## Fill these

**What good looks like**
- What does a strong product direction look like here, in a way you would defend against a smart
  person who disagrees?
- What does "decision-ready" mean to *you*, specifically — beyond what the brief says?

**What you kill on sight**
- What makes you reject a product idea instantly, before analysis?
- What patterns do you keep seeing in this product's work that you're tired of?

**Your standing trade-offs**
- Which trade-off do you take almost every time? (simplicity over configurability, speed to value over completeness, …)
- Where do you break that rule?

**What outsiders get wrong**
- What is always true about your domain that someone new to it assumes wrongly?
- What does your core user persona actually do, versus what people think they do?

**Evidence**
- What kinds of evidence do you trust here, and what do you discount?
- What number has misled this team before?

**Where the job breaks**
- At which point in a product process does work at your company most often go wrong?
- If you could only be in the room for one stage of this pipeline, which one, and why?

## How this gets used

Every stage loads this file. Claude applies it as a constraint — challenging work that violates it,
and flagging when the house view itself looks like it's producing a blind spot rather than clarity.
