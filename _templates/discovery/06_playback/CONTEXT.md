## Hard rule
**No polished deck.** Use the actual work. If the pair starts building slides, stop them — the
brief rules it out, and a deck signals the artefact can't carry itself.

## The session ends in a file, not a feeling
A session that produces only slides or vibes has failed. It ends with the decision written down.

## Process
1. Prepare a sharp answer to each of the seven:
   1. What problem did you ultimately decide you are solving? *(one sentence)*
   2. What informed your thinking? *(evidence, not activity)*
   3. What alternatives did you seriously consider? *(and why each was killed)*
   4. What direction are you recommending, and why? *(trade-off stated)*
   5. Show us the proposed experience. *(the three frames)*
   6. What assumptions or risks remain? *(classified, not hidden)*
   7. What now needs working through with Engineering?
2. Prepare the eighth, which the brief omits and leadership will ask:
   **What does this domain look like in 12 months?** A direction with no horizon reinforces the
   exact problem this programme exists to fix. Draft it here; `08` builds it properly.
3. Structure for a decision: lead with the recommendation, not the journey. Put the trade-off on
   the table early. State plainly what decision is being asked for and what happens if it isn't made.
   Have the "if you say no" answer ready.

## Interrogate before the session
- What question are you hoping they don't ask? Prepare that one first.
- Where are you presenting activity as evidence?
- If they decide in three minutes, what did the other 40 add?

## Outputs
- `playback.md` → `output/` — answers to all eight, the decision requested, running order
- After the session: the decision made and conditions attached, appended to `../../_shared/decision-log.md`

## Human check
Write the decision into the log before leaving the room. A decision not written down did not happen.
